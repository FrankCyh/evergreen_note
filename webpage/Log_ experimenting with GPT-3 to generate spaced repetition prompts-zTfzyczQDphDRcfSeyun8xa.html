A flashcard consists of a question and an answer. To make a flashcard, ask a question about an important detail in a passage.

Passage: In a manner similar to the way conventional computers are made up of bits, quantum computers are made up of quantum bits, or qubits. Just like a bit, a qubit has a state. But whereas the state of a bit is a number (0 or 1), the state of a qubit is a vector. More specifically, the state of a qubit is a vector in a two-dimensional vector space.
Question: The state of a qubit is a two-dimensional v___.
Answer: vector

Passage: One way qubits are similar to bits: we’ve said absolutely nothing about what the qubit actually is, physically. Maybe the state of the qubit is being stored somehow on an electron, or a photon, or an atom. Or maybe it’s being stored in something stranger, perhaps inside some exotic particle or state of matter, even further removed from our everyday experience.
Question: What are three types of physical system which could potentially be used to store a qubit?
Answer: Examples include (other answers also fine): electron, photon, atom

Passage: More specifically, the state of a qubit is a vector in a two-dimensional vector space. This vector space is known as state space.
Question: What do we call the two-dimensional vector space where the state of a qubit lives?
Answer: state space

Passage: This notation with | and > is called the ket notation, and things like |0> are called kets. But don’t be thrown off by the unfamiliar terminology – a ket is just a vector, and when we say something is a ket, all we mean is that it’s a vector.
Question: We use the term ket interchangeably with the term ___.
Answer: vector

Passage: There’s a tension here that applies to many proposals to do quantum information processing, not just neutrinos. If we want to store the quantum state, then it’s helpful if our qubits only interact very weakly with other systems, so those systems don’t disrupt them. But if the qubits only interact weakly with other systems then that also makes it hard to manipulate the qubits. Thus, systems which make good quantum wires are often hard to build quantum gates for.
Question: What could be an issue with designing a quantum computer out of qubits that interact weakly with other systems?
Answer: We can’t build effective gates for those qubits, since they only interact weakly with other systems.

A pretty good conversation with Feynman:

The following is a conversation with Richard Feynman.

Me: Where do new ideas come from?
Richard Feynman: That is a very, very difficult question. What can I say? My own experience is this - that if I could give any beginning student any advice at all, it would be “Don’t try too hard”. If you only learn some principle, or if you only learn to solve some problem, that is not enough. You might just have learned to juggle with the problem instead of solving it. You have to have some idea how to find things out - and there are some hints that I can give about how to find things out. One is to read the oldest books first, instead of the newest books on a subject. Another hint is to try to find the answer by a principle, or at least get some idea what kind of answer you should get - then look in places where you might find it. And the most important one is that when you ask a question - don’t just say, “I don’t understand this”, but try to answer it. If you can’t answer it, at least you should know why you can’t answer it. And once you understand the reason that you can’t do that, you can go on from there. Another hint - it is very important in order to make progress, not to get discouraged by small things. Suppose you are trying to track down a difficulty in some experiment - you try this, you try that, and it doesn’t work. Then you are sorry that you have to stop and go home because it is such a beautiful evening, and you are all ready to go on working. Well, you shouldn’t do that - you should just say to yourself, “OK, the experiment didn’t work today - but it’s going to work tomorrow!” and then you should go on from there. The main thing is that you shouldn’t get discouraged if at first you don’t get very far. You see, the big mistake that people make is to assume that because they don’t understand, the problem is something deep, when it is only that they are not thinking hard enough - they are not solving the problem.

Me: What makes you happy?
Richard Feynman: I can’t answer that. It would be too long to write all the things. One of the reasons is that it is fun to discover things. Another reason is playing the bongo drums. And another reason is that it is pleasant to be with people that I love.
